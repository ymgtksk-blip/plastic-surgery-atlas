Genealogy of Reconstructive Operations

From Sushruta in 600 BCE to the present — the original papers on the procedures, flaps, skin grafts and microsurgery that shaped plastic surgery, and the people who first performed them, traced in chronological order with links to primary sources.

c. 600 BCELocal flaps & flap principles1st

Cheek flap — the origin of Indian rhinoplasty

Sushruta cheek flap (Sushruta Samhita)

Sushruta

Sushruta Samhita

Ancient Indian surgical treatise (manuscript)

The first written description in history of the fundamental principle of flap surgery: reconstructing a defect with a pedicled local flap (a flap transferred while leaving a pedicle that preserves its blood supply).

※ Historical sources vary

Historical note: background to the “world first”

The oldest recorded flap reconstruction in the world. It describes reconstructing a nose cut off as a punishment with a pedicled cheek flap, using a leaf to make a template (pattern). It is regarded as the source of the later “Indian” forehead flap. It is widely estimated to have been composed around the 6th century BCE, but the date of composition and the interpretation of the original text vary among historical sources.

Dorsal metacarpal artery perforator (DMAP) flap

A distally based island flap pedicled on a single cutaneous perforator that is constantly present at the metacarpal neck (about 0.5–1 cm proximal to the MP joint), without dissecting the main trunk of the dorsal metacarpal artery. It covers defects of the dorsal fingers, web spaces and dorsum of the hand with thin tissue and little invasiveness, and, commonly called the Quaba flap, is one of the workhorse flaps of hand reconstruction.

Historical note: background to the “world first”

Building on the anatomical evidence of Earley & Milner (1987), dorsal metacarpal artery flaps were reported in quick succession in 1990 by Maruyama (the reverse type, which uses the main trunk with reverse flow) and by Quaba & Davison (the distally based type raised on a single direct cutaneous perforator). The latter became linked with Koshima’s (1989) perforator flap concept and came to be called the DMAP flap = Quaba flap. The perforators are constant at the metacarpal necks of the second to fourth web spaces.

Keystone perforator island flap

Keystone Design Perforator Island Flap (Behan)

A perforator-dependent island flap shaped like an arched trapezoid (keystone) that allows simple, minimally invasive primary closure of defects anywhere from the scalp to the foot.

Historical note: background to the “world first”

The original paper that first described the concept of the “keystone flap.” Drawing on experience with 300 cases over the preceding seven years, it presented five cases and became a new option for reconstruction after excision of skin cancer.

Free-style perforator flap

Fu-Chan Wei & Samir Mardini (free-style free flap)

Established the “free-style” technique of raising a flap from any site, relying only on a perforator signal confirmed by preoperative Doppler.

Historical note: background to the “world first”

Proposed the free-style concept of raising a flap starting from a perforator, unconstrained by known anatomy (PRS 2004;114(4):910-916). It greatly expanded the freedom of perforator flaps.
